## DesigXner POV (the opinions)

1. **The freelancer tier is underrated.** The internet is full of agency-written content telling you freelancers are risky. For an established service business spending $3,500–$6,000 with a vetted freelancer, you typically get better attention, faster communication, and comparable output to a $12,000 agency project — because you're not subsidising account management overhead. The risk is real but manageable with a proper contract and a staged payment schedule.

2. **Cheap websites don't save money — they defer the bill with interest.** The cost of a website that underperforms isn't the $500 you spent — it's the 18 months of poor lead flow before you rebuild it. When you account for missed leads and the cost of the second build, the "budget" option reliably costs more than doing it right the first time. The total cost of ownership comparison is what matters, not the upfront number.

3. **"How much does a website cost?" is the wrong first question.** The right question is "what should a website produce, and what is a lead worth to my business?" A $10,000 website that generates 15 qualified leads per month at a 30% close rate is the cheapest marketing channel most service businesses have ever used. A $500 website that generates nothing is infinitely more expensive.

## FAQs to include (emit as schema)

- **Q: How much does a small business website cost in 2026?**
  A: It depends on who builds it. A DIY builder (Wix, Squarespace) runs $240–$600/year. A freelance designer charges $1,500–$8,000 as a one-time project fee. A boutique agency typically charges $6,000–$20,000. Custom builds start at $20,000. Most established small businesses investing in a professional site land between $4,000 and $12,000 all-in for the first year.

- **Q: What are the ongoing costs of a small business website?**
  A: Plan for $1,100–$5,000/year in ongoing costs: hosting ($50–$500/year), maintenance ($500–$3,600/year for professional plans), domain renewal ($10–$20/year), and any paid plugins or tools. Content updates and SEO are additional if outsourced.

- **Q: Is a cheap website worth it for a small business?**
  A: Only if your business is pre-revenue or genuinely experimental. For an established business, a cheap website typically triggers a rebuild within 18–24 months. When you add the rebuild cost, the missed leads, and the time you spent managing it, the "cheap" option usually costs more over three years than investing properly the first time.

- **Q: How much should I budget for a small business website?**
  A: If you're a service business generating more than $100k/year in revenue, budget at least $3,500–$6,000 for a freelance build or $8,000–$15,000 for an agency build. If the website is your primary lead channel, treat it as a $10,000–$20,000 investment amortised over three years — roughly $280–$550/month — and measure it against your cost per lead.

- **Q: What's not included in most website quotes?**
  A: Copywriting, photography, SEO setup, hosting, ongoing maintenance, and any third-party integrations (booking tools, payment processors, CRM connectors) are routinely excluded from headline quotes. Always ask for an itemised breakdown and specifically ask "what does this quote not include?"
